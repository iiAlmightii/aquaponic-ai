On Sarvam 30B for crop intelligence + financial planning:

This is a genuinely good idea and I'd encourage it. Here's my quick take:

Sarvam 30B being free removes the cost barrier entirely, and it's trained with Indian agricultural and economic context baked in — which is actually more relevant for your domain (lakh/crore financial framing, Indian crops, Indian climate zones) than a generic western LLM.

The main tradeoffs to think through:

Crop intelligence: Right now it's static research-paper logic. Replacing it with a chat-capable model means users get dynamic Q&A ("why is my DO dropping?", "what's the best fish density for my tank size?") rather than canned recommendations. This is a real qualitative leap.
Financial planning: The current engine is a deterministic calculator (CAPEX/OPEX/ROI/IRR). Sarvam 30B could wrap it — interpret free-form financial questions, explain the scenarios in plain language, and answer follow-ups. The hard numbers still come from your calculator; the LLM adds conversational reasoning on top.
Paper angle: "We replaced static heuristics with a domain-appropriate Indian LLM (Sarvam 30B)" is a much stronger IEEE contribution than just the STT comparison. Two empirical contributions in one paper is solid.
